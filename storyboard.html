Galaxy Sort

A 4-phase classroom activity. Students sort galaxies their own way, share with their table, learn the three types from you, then sort again and see how Mr. Gupta sorted them, and why. Every screen below uses the real styles, images and wording.

Changes since v0.1

  • Plainer tone. Emoji and party language are gone. Encouragement stays, but it's matter-of-fact ("Solid work", not "You think like an astronomer!").
  • New font: Inter (it was Baloo 2/Nunito). Corners are squarer, buttons are rectangles instead of pills, and the magnifying-glass emoji is now a plain expand icon.
  • Back button on "Eyes up front", so students who skip ahead can return to their groups.
  • Passcode is BUBBLE (case doesn't matter).
  • Word filter on group names and "why" text.
  • No teacher/projector view. You'll use your own slides.
  • Clue text is less exclamatory. Your M74 wording ("See the arms winding out…") is kept.

Phase 1 · Free sort

  • Two ways to move a galaxy: drag it (mouse or finger), or tap it (gold outline) and then tap a group. Tapping is the reliable option on phones.
  • Starts with 2 empty groups plus "+ New group". ✕ deletes a group and sends its galaxies back to the top.
  • The corner icon on a tile opens a large view of the image.
  • Galaxy names are hidden until the results screen.
  • Gate: every galaxy placed, at least 2 groups.

Phase 1b · Name + why (required)

  • Every group needs a name and a reason of at least 3 characters. Any empty field is outlined in gold.
  • Word filter: names and reasons are checked against a short list of profanity and slurs. A match is replaced with *** as the student types, before it can reach the share screen. Students can get around it with creative spelling, but casual cases are caught.
  • "Back to sorting" keeps everything.

Phase 4 · Results

  • The headline is "You and Mr. Gupta agreed on N of 11." One line of feedback underneath, depending on N:
    • 11: "You matched every one."
    • 8–10: "Solid work. Here's where you sorted differently, and what to look for."
    • 5–7: "Good start. The ones below are where the clues are easy to miss."
    • 0–4: "These are tricky. Here's what to look for in each one."
  • Disagreements come first, each with the image, both choices and the clue. Matches are a compact row with check marks.
  • The two tricky galaxies get an extra line saying astronomers debate them too.
  • Link back to the first sort: any of the student's groups that is mostly one type gets matched to that type by name.
  • "Start over" asks for confirmation before erasing. You can also open …/?reset on any device.
